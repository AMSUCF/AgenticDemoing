# Butterfly Image Catalog

## 1. Spicebush Swallowtail (*Papilio troilus*)
**File:** `spicebush-swallowtail.png`

A Spicebush Swallowtail resting on green grass with its wings spread open. The butterfly displays its characteristic dark black wings adorned with iridescent blue scaling on the hindwings, rows of cream-white marginal spots, and a small orange spot near the base of the tail. This species is commonly found in eastern North America and is named after its larval host plant, the spicebush.

## 2. Eastern Tiger Swallowtail (*Papilio glaucus*)
**File:** `eastern-tiger-swallowtail.png`

An Eastern Tiger Swallowtail perched on a branch against a bright blue sky, surrounded by delicate pink blossoms and green foliage. The butterfly shows its bold yellow wings marked with prominent black tiger-like stripes and dark wing borders. This is one of the most recognizable and widespread swallowtail species in eastern North America.

## 3. Painted Lady (*Vanessa cardui*)
**File:** `painted-lady.png`

A Painted Lady butterfly sitting on white snapdragon flowers speckled with rain droplets. The wings display the species' distinctive patchwork of orange, brown, black, and white markings, with dark spots scattered across the lower wings. The Painted Lady is one of the most widely distributed butterflies in the world, found on every continent except Antarctica and Australia.

## 4. White Peacock (*Anartia jatrophae*)
**File:** `white-peacock.png`

A White Peacock butterfly resting on a green leaf with its wings fully spread. The wings are pale grayish-white with subtle brown and orange banding along the edges and small, round eyespots on both the forewings and hindwings. This tropical and subtropical species is commonly found in the southern United States, Central America, and South America.

## 5. Monarch (*Danaus plexippus*)
**File:** `monarch.png`

A Monarch butterfly feeding on bright orange butterfly weed (milkweed) flowers amid lush green foliage. The iconic orange wings are outlined with bold black veins and bordered with black edges dotted with white spots. Monarchs are famous for their extraordinary multi-generational migration spanning thousands of miles between North America and central Mexico.

## 6. Zebra Longwing (*Heliconius charithonia*)
**File:** `zebra-longwing.png`

A Zebra Longwing butterfly with its elongated wings spread open, perched among green leaves in warm light. The wings are jet black with striking pale yellow longitudinal stripes, giving the species its zebra-like name. This is the state butterfly of Florida and is notable for its ability to feed on pollen as well as nectar, granting it an unusually long lifespan for a butterfly.

## 7. Ceraunus Blue (*Hemiargus ceraunus*)
**File:** `ceraunus-blue.png`

A tiny Ceraunus Blue butterfly clinging to the side of a green plant spike, photographed in profile. The underwings display an intricate pattern of wavy brown and white lines with a prominent blue-ringed black eyespot near the hindwing tail. This small lycaenid butterfly is found across the southern United States, the Caribbean, and Central and South America.

## 8. Cabbage White (*Pieris rapae*)
**File:** `cabbage-white.png`

A Cabbage White butterfly perched on a green leaf with its wings folded upward, revealing the pale underside. The wings are a soft creamy white with faint yellowish-green veining visible beneath. Originally from Europe, this species is now one of the most common butterflies worldwide and is frequently seen in gardens, fields, and agricultural areas where its caterpillars feed on plants in the cabbage family.
